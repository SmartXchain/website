ASML reported €8.8 billion in Q1 2026 revenue — up 13 percent year-over-year — and raised its full-year outlook to €36–40 billion. Gross margin hit 53 percent, the top end of guidance. Net income was €2.8 billion. By every conventional measure, this was an exceptional quarter from the most important company in the global semiconductor supply chain.

The stock fell.

That gap between strong results and a falling share price is the most interesting signal in the AI capex story right now. Understanding why requires understanding what ASML actually sells and who is buying it.

## The company most people have never heard of

ASML makes the machines that make chips. Specifically, it makes the extreme ultraviolet lithography machines — EUV systems — that are the only way to manufacture chips at 5nm, 3nm, and 2nm process nodes. There is no alternative supplier. TSMC, Samsung, and Intel cannot build leading-edge chips without ASML's machines. A single EUV system costs approximately €200 million and takes 18 months to manufacture and install. ASML ships roughly 60 EUV systems per year. ASML's next-generation platform — High-NA EUV, required for 2nm and below — costs over €350 million per unit.

This makes ASML's order book the most honest forward-looking indicator of where semiconductor manufacturing investment is actually going. Capital expenditure announcements from chipmakers are often vague and subject to revision. An ASML purchase order is a specific commitment: a specific machine, a specific facility, a specific timeline. When ASML raises its full-year outlook, it is not expressing optimism about AI — it is reporting purchase commitments already in the queue.

## What the raised outlook tells you about AI infrastructure

The €36–40 billion full-year guidance represents a meaningful acceleration from 2025. The driver is well-understood: AI model training and inference requires leading-edge logic chips at enormous scale, and that scale is being built now. NVIDIA's H200 and B200 accelerators are manufactured on TSMC's 4nm and 3nm processes. The next generation of AI chips — from NVIDIA, Google, Amazon, Microsoft, and the model labs themselves — will require 2nm. 2nm chips require High-NA EUV. High-NA EUV is ASML.

The raised outlook is effectively a leading indicator that the hyperscalers and chipmakers have committed to a volume of 2nm production that requires ASML machines currently in production. Given the 18-month manufacturing and installation timeline, those commitments translate to AI infrastructure that will come online in 2027 and 2028. The AI capex cycle has runway well past the current quarter.

## The export control variable

ASML's guidance explicitly accounts for "possible outcomes related to ongoing export control discussions." This is the diplomatic phrasing for a genuine constraint: Dutch and US export controls restrict ASML from shipping EUV systems to China. China represents a meaningful portion of the semiconductor equipment market. The restrictions have pushed Chinese chipmakers to buy older deep ultraviolet DUV systems at scale — ASML is still selling to China, just not its most advanced equipment.

This creates an interesting structural dynamic. The China market drives DUV revenue; the rest of the world drives EUV and High-NA EUV revenue. As the technology generation relevant to AI moves firmly into EUV territory, the China exposure becomes less financially significant to ASML's growth story, even as it remains politically significant. The raised outlook is driven by EUV demand from customers where export controls do not apply.

## Why the stock fell

The quarter was strong. The guidance was strong. The stock fell because the expectations embedded in ASML's valuation — trading at roughly 30x forward earnings before the results — already priced in strong quarters and rising guidance. When the actual results arrive and they are strong but not transcendent, the multiple compresses.

This is the classic paradox of quality compounders at peak-cycle valuations. The business is executing well. The investment case requires the business to execute better than the market's already-aggressive assumptions. That is a different bar, and ASML did not clear it on this quarter's results.

The more interesting question is not whether ASML beat this quarter but what the order book implies about the AI capex cycle in 2027 and 2028. On that question, the answer from the quarter is unambiguously bullish. The commitments are in place. The machines are in production. The infrastructure is coming.

## The honest read

ASML's order book is as close as you can get to a ground-truth view of AI infrastructure investment, stripped of the press release noise and quarterly earnings management that surrounds hyperscaler capex announcements. What it shows is a multi-year buildout that is accelerating, not decelerating. The stock price reaction this quarter is a valuation story, not a business story. Those are different things, and conflating them is the most common mistake in semiconductor equipment investing.
