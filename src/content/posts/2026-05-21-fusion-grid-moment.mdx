The standard line on commercial fusion is "wonderful, twenty years away." That line has been correct for sixty years. It is, however, finally about to stop being correct — and the timeline is much tighter than the policy people realize.

## Three things happened this month

**One:** Helion signed its first commercial PPA. Microsoft is the buyer. Fifty megawatts, target online date 2028, contracted-but-conditional pricing competitive with utility-scale solar plus storage. This is the first PPA in history priced on actual fusion economics, not subsidies.

**Two:** The Department of Energy quietly updated NRC pre-licensing guidance to clarify that magnetic-confinement and inertial-confinement systems below a defined activation threshold do not require fission-class licensing. This sounds technical. It is technical. It is also worth roughly two years of timeline and several hundred million dollars per project.

**Three:** A new chart started circulating in utility-sector slack channels. It overlays small-modular-fission timelines (pre- and post- the 2023 Vogtle disaster) with fusion timelines. The fusion curve has crossed the SMR curve. Whichever curve you trust, fusion is now the bet with the better risk-adjusted timeline.

## Why utility CFOs should care

Most utility long-range planning still assumes fusion is a 2040+ option. If it lands in 2028–2030 — even at small scale, even at high prices — every long-duration storage investment made in the last five years has to be re-underwritten. The asset-life math gets ugly fast.

This is why the smart utility executives I talk to are not asking "is fusion real?" They're asking: "How quickly can I write a 5MW pilot PPA so I'm not the last one to learn?"

## The honest caveat

The first commercial fusion plant will be late, over budget, and underperform its spec. That is true of every first-of-kind plant in energy history. It will still arrive a decade earlier than the conventional wisdom assumed. That's the part nobody priced in.
